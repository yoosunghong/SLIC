

# Paper Review: Routing Legibility in Cooperative MARL

**Target Workshop:** NExT-Game 2026 (ICML 2026 Workshop)
**Review Target:** Routing Legibility in Cooperative MARL: Measuring the Translation Gap Between Theory-Informed Roles and Learned Conventions (6 pages, under review)
**Review Date:** 2026-05-06

***

## 1. Paper Summary

**Core Contribution.** An analytical framework for diagnosing the "translation gap" (theoretical role prescription ↔ learned convention) — consisting of (i) role-routing matrix, (ii) formation sensitivity Δ_max, and (iii) gradient/occlusion attribution.

**Key Findings.**

- Attention policies generate far more structured role-conditioned routing than MLP (routing entry std 0.275 vs. 0.055).
- Removing role labels from self/ally tokens results in a 17.8 pp drop in win rate.
- 3v3 training checkpoints transfer zero-shot to 9v9 (0.213 vs. from-scratch ~0.15).
- 2 of 4 pre-registered theoretical predictions were revised by the data — this constitutes the core empirical evidence of the translation gap.

***

## 2. Major Strengths

1. **Elegant research framing.** The "translation gap" concept aligns well with the workshop's theme of game-theoretic learning, and the paper differentiates itself by treating legibility itself as the measurement target rather than a simple performance comparison.
2. **Falsifiable prior predictions stated upfront.** Pre-registering the testable predictions in Section 3 and honestly marking them PASS / REVISED — this is the paper's most powerful asset.
3. **Multi-metric triangulation.** Cross-validating attention weights, gradient attribution, and occlusion sensitivity (Pearson r = 0.885) avoids dependence on a single measurement.
4. **Confound control.** The padding-confound ablation (Table 4) quantifies the mask effect and derives architectural residual ≥ 85% — preemptively blocking the first thing a reviewer would suspect.
5. **Cross-domain replication.** Qualitative patterns replicate across MiniGrid + SMACv2 domains, but preferred partner roles differ — consistent with the claim that "architecture governs legibility, environment determines which convention stabilizes."
6. **Does not shy away from negative results.** Repositioning predictions (3) and (4) being wrong as the central result of the paper is academically honest.

***

## 3. Major Weaknesses / Improvement Recommendations

### A. Insufficient Statistical Power (Most Urgent)

- **3 seeds is insufficient even for a workshop.** Looking at Table 1, C2 (0.551 ± 0.210) and C4 (0.474 ± 0.204) — the standard deviation is comparable to or larger than the point estimate effect size. The 21.2 pp difference between C1 and C3 (0.652 vs. 0.440) likely has overlapping 95% CIs.
- **Improvement recommendation.** Expand to at least 5 seeds; add statistical tests such as paired bootstrap or permutation test. If this is not feasible, explicitly stating the limitations of statistical power in the Limitations section is the safer approach.


### B. Weak Derivation of Theoretical Predictions

- The rationale for prediction (3) "Δ_max is highest for Vanguard" and (4) "Strike → Support" in Section 3 is nearly absent from the main text. It is unclear whether this is mere intuition ("Vanguard is frontline, so...") or something derived from classical game theory.
- **If this is weak, the meaning of "translation gap" is diluted.** Stating in 1–2 paragraphs why these predictions should have held is what makes "predictions were wrong = gap" convincing.
- **Improvement recommendation.** Add a "Why these predictions" paragraph to Section 3. Show how predictions are derived in a non-ad-hoc way from role-specific reward shaping formulations.


### C. Notation Issues in Attention Formula

- **The value matrix V is missing from the cross-attention formula in Section 3.** Standard attention is softmax(QK^T / √d_k) V, but the formula in the main text only defines the attention weight. If intentional (analyzing only the weights themselves), this must be explicitly stated.
- Additionally, it is stated that Q comes from self_tok and K/V from ally_tok — yet Figure 1 shows cross-attention also over enemy/zone tokens. Consistency needs to be checked: is self only the query, or is the entire entity-set the query?


### D. Ambiguous Distinction Between C5 and C4

- C4 No-Role (shared): "C1 with role one-hots zeroed in self and all ally slots, single shared policy across roles."
- C5 Flat MLP: "C3 with role one-hots zeroed."
- The two conditions appear nearly identical (role-removed baselines). The difference — whether it is (a) shared vs. independent policy or (b) presence/absence of attention — needs to be clearly separated and rewritten. The fact that C4 (0.474) and C5 (0.494) in Table 1 look statistically nearly identical is also entangled with this definitional ambiguity.


### E. No Quantitative Definition of "Translation Gap"

- The concept is attractive, but throughout the paper there is no quantitative metric other than "2 of 4 predictions were revised."
- **Improvement recommendation.** In the Discussion, propose candidate metrics for future work — e.g., KL / Frobenius distance between theoretical prior and learned routing matrix, prediction calibration score, etc.


### F. Risk in Interpreting 9v9 Results

- Table 2: 9v9 C1 win rate 0.140 ± 0.110, C3 is 0.077 ± 0.021. Both comparisons are in very low-performing regimes, making the claim that "structural advantage is preserved" based on a weak signal.
- "Scaling preserves routing signature" is bolstered by routing analysis, but readers looking only at win rates may suspect "didn't both fail to learn?" Showing AUC together is good, but a training-time scaling ablation showing whether the 9v9 learning curve saturated would be better.


### G. Sparse Related Work

- 7 references is too few even for a workshop. Notably missing areas:
    - **Role learning in MARL:** RODE (Wang et al. 2021), LDSA, ROGC, etc.
    - **Attention in MARL:** Beyond MAAC — transformer-based MARL such as UPDeT.
    - **Convention learning / equilibrium selection:** Other-Play (Hu et al. 2020), Off-Belief Learning.
- Adding one more paragraph to Section 2 would reduce the room for reviewers to suspect "selectivity."

## 5. Workshop Fit (NExT-Game / ICML)

- **Fit: Good.** "Convention selection" and "equilibrium vs. learned solution" are core themes of game-theoretic learning. However, game theory analysis per se (e.g., Schelling points, focal points, correlated equilibrium) appears only superficially.
- **Recommendation.** Add one paragraph in the Introduction or Discussion reinforcing the connection to game theory concepts — e.g., framing such as "the learned routing convention can be viewed as a focal point selected by gradient dynamics among multiple equilibria consistent with the role structure."

***

## 6. Overall Assessment

**Current State.** As a workshop paper, this is at the **borderline accept ~ weak accept** level. Concept, analytical tools, and honest negative result reporting are strengths; weak statistical power and theoretical derivation are risk factors.

**Priority Revision Suggestions:**

1. **(Essential)** Add 1 paragraph on prediction derivation in Section 3.
2. **(Essential)** Clarify C4 vs. C5 definitions in Section 4; explicitly include the V matrix in the attention formula.
3. **(Recommended)** Expand to 5 seeds + add paired tests for key comparisons.
4. **(Recommended)** Add 3–5 references on role learning / convention learning to Related Work.
5. **(Recommended)** Add one paragraph on candidate quantitative metrics for "translation gap" in the Discussion.

**The biggest asset is positioning "2 of 4 predictions were wrong" as the conclusion.** If this honesty is drawn out more boldly as the narrative thread throughout the entire paper (consistent emphasis: Abstract → Intro → Discussion), there is a good chance the reviewer score will improve by one level.

*End of Review.*
